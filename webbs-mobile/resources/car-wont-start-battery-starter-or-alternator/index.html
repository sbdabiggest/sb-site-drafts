Car won't start: is it the battery, the starter or the alternator?

The three-minute check anyone can do before calling, and what each fix runs.

Rapid clicking with dim lights = battery. One loud click or nothing with bright lights = starter. Started fine, then died while driving with a battery light = alternator. All three are driveway repairs; the check below tells you which one before anyone drives out.

Florida heat kills batteries faster than anywhere up north; three years is a good run down here. But a car that won't start isn't always the battery, and a jump on a bad alternator just strands you again a mile down Beal. Here is how to tell them apart.

The three-minute check

  1. Turn the key to ON (not start). Dash lights dim or dead? Battery. Bright? Keep going.
  2. Try to start it. Rapid click-click-click: battery too weak to turn the starter. One heavy clunk or silence with bright lights: starter (or a corroded connection at the starter). Cranks strong but never catches: not the battery, not the starter; that's fuel or spark, a different call.
  3. Did it die while driving? Battery light or dimming headlights before it quit means the alternator stopped charging. A jump will start it and it will die again.
  4. Look at the terminals. White or green crust on the battery posts can cause every symptom above. Sometimes the fix is a cleaning.

What each fix runs in the driveway

  • Battery: the part is the price ($150–$300 for most cars in 2026) plus a short install. Bring your own battery and the labor is small.
  • Starter: parts vary by car; labor is 1–2 hours on most, more if it's buried. Still a driveway job.
  • Alternator: similar to a starter on most cars; on some engines it's a longer reach. You'll get the number before the work starts.
  • Terminals/cables: the cheap fix people miss.

When not to jump it

If the battery is swollen, leaking, or smells like rotten eggs, don't jump it and don't drive it. If it died while driving with the battery light on, a jump gets you a mile. And if the starter is the problem, a jump does nothing at all; that's the one people spend an hour on before calling.

Frequently asked questions

How long do batteries last in Florida?

Two to four years. Heat is harder on them than cold. If yours is three summers old and the car cranks slow in the morning, it's telling you.

Can you test it before replacing anything?

Yes. Battery, alternator output and starter draw all get tested on site before a part is recommended.

Do you come out if it's just a battery?

Yes. It's the most common call and usually done within the hour of arriving.
