What is birria, and how do you eat quesabirria tacos?

The dish, the dip, and a first order that makes sense, from the truck on Mobile Highway.

Birria is beef (traditionally goat) slow-cooked for hours in a chile and spice broth until it falls apart. A quesabirria taco is that meat with melted cheese in a tortilla dipped in the fat of the broth and griddled crisp. The cup of red broth on the side is consomé: you dip the taco in it, bite, repeat. You don't drink it first; you dip.

If you found us from a photo of red tacos and a cup of broth, this is the page. Here is what birria is, why it's red, what the cup is for, and what to order the first time you pull up to Mobile Highway.

The dish

Birria comes from Jalisco. Meat cooks low and slow in a broth of dried chiles (guajillo, ancho), garlic, cumin, oregano, bay and vinegar until it shreds with a fork. The broth that's left is the consomé, and the red fat on top of it is what gets the tortillas their color and crunch on the griddle.

How to eat quesabirria

  1. Pick up the taco. It's crisp on the outside, cheesy inside.
  2. Dip the corner in the consomé. Not a swim, a dip.
  3. Bite. Dip again. Squeeze lime, add onion and cilantro between bites.
  4. When the tacos are gone, drink what's left of the consomé. That's dessert.

Napkins: more than you think.

A first order

  • Three quesabirria tacos with consomé is the classic first plate.
  • Birria ramen if it's a cool night: the consomé with noodles, meat and onion.
  • Birria pizza for a table: the one the pictures are about.
  • A cup of consomé to go works as soup on its own.

Heat level: warm, not spicy. The salsa on the side is where the heat lives, so you control it.

Frequently asked questions

Is birria spicy?

The meat and consomé are warm and savory, not hot. The salsa is where the heat is, and it's optional.

Beef or goat?

Beef. Traditional birria is goat; most trucks in the US, including this one, cook beef because it's what people expect and it's incredible.

When is the truck open?

Tuesday through Saturday from 4 pm at 4700 Mobile Hwy, Pensacola. Closed Sunday and Monday. Check Facebook for holidays.
