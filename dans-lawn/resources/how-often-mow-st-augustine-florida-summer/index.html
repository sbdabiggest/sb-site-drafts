How often should you mow St. Augustine grass in the Florida summer?

Weekly is the honest answer from May to September, and cutting height matters more than most people think.

In the Emerald Coast summer, St. Augustine needs mowing every 5–7 days, cut at 3.5 to 4 inches, never taking off more than a third of the blade. Letting it go two weeks then scalping it is what causes the brown, thin patches people blame on bugs or the sun.

Most of the yards we cut in Mary Esther and Fort Walton Beach are St. Augustine. It loves our heat and rain, which is exactly why it gets away from people between May and September. Here is the schedule and height that keep it thick without turning the summer into a fight.

The summer schedule

  • May through September: every 5–7 days. After a week of afternoon storms it can put on two inches.
  • March–April and October: every 7–10 days as growth ramps up or slows down.
  • November through February: every 2–3 weeks, mostly to keep it tidy and pick up leaves. It barely grows.

The rule under all of it: never remove more than a third of the blade in one cut. If the grass is 6 inches tall, cutting it to 4 is fine; cutting it to 2.5 stresses it, exposes the runners, and opens the door for weeds and chinch bugs.

Cutting height is the part people get wrong

St. Augustine wants to be tall. Set the mower at 3.5 to 4 inches in summer. Shorter looks neat for a day, then the sun cooks the soil, the grass thins, and the brown patches show up. Taller grass shades its own roots, holds water between storms, and crowds out weeds.

Keep blades sharp. A dull blade tears the leaf and leaves a grey-brown cast over the whole yard a day after mowing.

Why the yard browns out even when it's watered

  • Scalping after a skipped week (see above).
  • Chinch bugs in the hottest, driest spots, usually along driveways and sidewalks. The patch spreads outward and the grass pulls up easily.
  • Fungus after wet weeks — round patches with a smoky ring, common after a stretch of rain and heat.
  • Mowing wet — clumps smother the grass underneath.

Frequently asked questions

Should I bag or mulch the clippings?

Mulch. Fine clippings feed the lawn and disappear in a day or two when you're mowing on schedule. Bag only when the grass got away from you and the clippings would clump.

How much should I water in summer?

About an inch a week including rain, applied in one or two deep waterings early in the morning, not a little every day. In a normal Gulf Coast summer, rain covers most of it.

Is weekly mowing worth paying for?

For St. Augustine in summer, yes — it's the difference between a yard that stays thick and one that gets scalped and patchy every other week. Off-season, bi-weekly is fine and we'll tell you when to switch.
