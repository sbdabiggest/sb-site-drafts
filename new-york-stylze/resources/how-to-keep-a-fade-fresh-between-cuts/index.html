How to keep a fade fresh between cuts

The at-home routine that stretches a fade, and the two things that ruin one early.

A fade stays sharp about two weeks; a lineup at the two-week mark and the right daily routine stretches the full cut to three or four. Brush or sponge the top daily, keep the scalp moisturized, sleep on satin or a durag, and never let anyone but your barber touch the hairline.

The cut looks perfect walking out the door. Two weeks later the hairline is fuzzy and the blend has grown into a shadow. Some of that is just hair growing; some of it is avoidable. Here is the routine we tell every client.

Daily

  • Brush or sponge the top for a minute: curls stack, waves lay down, and the fade underneath keeps its shape.
  • Moisturize the scalp lightly. Dry scalp flakes at the skin line and makes a clean fade look grey.
  • Satin pillowcase or a durag at night. Cotton pulls moisture and frizzes the top; that's what makes a cut look old by day ten.

The two things that ruin a fade early

  1. A home lineup. Every barber has fixed a hairline that a friend "just touched up" with clippers. The line moves back a quarter inch each time and there's no putting it back. If the edge bothers you, book a 10-minute lineup.
  2. Heavy product on the fade itself. Grease and pomade belong on top. On the sides they attract lint and make the blend look dirty.

The schedule that works

Full cut, then a lineup around day 12–14, then a full cut at week 3 or 4. The lineup costs a fraction of the cut and buys you the second half of the month looking like the first. Book both at once on Booksy so the slot is there.

Frequently asked questions

Should I wash my hair every day?

Two or three times a week with a gentle shampoo is enough for most. Daily washing dries the scalp and shows at the fade line.

Does a wave brush help?

For waves, yes: brush in the direction of growth, morning and night, with a durag at night. The fade underneath stays clean either way.

How long does a lineup take?

About ten minutes. It's the best value in the shop.
